# Understanding and Predicting Online News Popularity

**ES 335 · Goutham (25110359) and Adimulam Mokshajna Sai Satya Sri Harsha (25110011)**

We study whether article-level features improve predictions of recorded social shares for later Mashable articles compared with a constant baseline. The project compares a median baseline, Ridge regression, random forest and boosted trees using a chronological validation/test design. The selected forest provides a modest improvement; highly shared articles remain difficult to predict. The static website presents the reasoning and an interactive promotion-budget exercise using held-out predictions.

**Live website:** https://goutham018-maker.github.io/Online-news-Popularity-ML/#home

## Evaluation protocol

- **Split:** sorted unique publication dates are divided at indices floor(0.6D) and floor(0.8D); every article from a date stays together. Training has 21,892 articles (2013-01-07–2014-03-14), validation 8,440 (2014-03-15–2014-08-05), and test 9,312 (2014-08-06–2014-12-27). There is no random row shuffle or target stratification. Exact row IDs are in `results/splits.csv`.
- **Features:** 41 predictors remain after excluding URL, timedelta, nine keyword share summaries, three referenced-article share summaries and five original LDA topics. Exclusions reflect publication-time availability or unknown fitting provenance; the target and split metadata are never inputs.
- **Preprocessing:** strip column-name whitespace; replace nonfinite inputs and out-of-range cells in three bounded word-rate fields with missing values. Median imputation is fitted on the fitting partition only. Ridge alone also uses training-fitted standardization. No original rows or extreme targets are removed. Models fit ln(1 + shares); expm1 predictions are clipped at zero.
- **Selection:** each of seven candidate configurations fits on training only. Validation log MAE selects the settings within each family and the overall winner. The full grid is in `results/validation_trials.json` and the source. Random seed is 335.
- **Final refit:** with choices frozen, each model refits on training + validation, including preprocessing. Final test data are used only for reporting and diagnostics. The baseline minimizes absolute log error; learned models fit squared log error. No post-inversion bias correction is applied.
- **Metrics:** log MAE is primary; RMSLE, raw-share MAE and log-space R² provide complementary views. Lower errors are better. Inverting a mean log prediction does not estimate the raw conditional mean.
- **Uncertainty:** the paired-day bootstrap resamples test dates 2,000 times (seed 335), comparing baseline-minus-forest log MAE. It does not refit models, model between-day serial dependence or quantify future distribution shifts.

## Main results

| Model | Validation log MAE | Test log MAE | Test RMSLE | Test MAE (shares) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Median baseline | 0.6433 | 0.6567 | 0.9102 | 2,153 |
| Ridge | 0.6060 | 0.6387 | 0.8629 | 2,131 |
| Random forest | 0.6038 | 0.6317 | 0.8541 | 2,121 |
| Boosted trees | 0.6060 | 0.6334 | 0.8547 | 2,124 |

The validation-selected forest improves test log MAE by **3.8%**, while raw-share MAE improves by only **1.5%**. The paired-day bootstrap 95% interval for the absolute log-MAE improvement is **0.0182–0.0325**. For the 444 test articles with at least 10,000 observed shares, the median outcome is 17,150 versus a median prediction of about 2,032.

Labels are cumulative snapshots, not fixed-horizon outcomes. Earlier training labels may include shares accumulated after later articles were published. This is therefore a retrospective chronological benchmark, not a fully timestamped deployment simulation. Results concern one publisher in 2013–2014; they do not establish causal effects or dependable virality forecasts.

## Interactive promotion exercise

Choose a model, channel, promotion budget and popularity threshold. The model promotes the top ceil(N × budget/100) articles by predicted shares. Evaluation reports precision, recall and lift over expected random selection, alongside a gains curve and tables of selected or missed popular articles. Observed shares score the ranking after selection; they never determine the model's shortlist.

Default settings (forest, all channels, 10% budget, at least 5,000 shares) promote 932 of 9,312 articles and capture 236 of 1,047 popular articles: precision **25.32%**, recall **22.54%**, lift **2.25×**. The constant baseline has no ranking signal, so it uses expected random tie-breaking rather than an arbitrary unique shortlist. Learned-model ties break by original row ID. This is a retrospective pooled-candidate exercise with equal promotion costs, not evidence that promotion causes extra shares or a new test-tuned model.
